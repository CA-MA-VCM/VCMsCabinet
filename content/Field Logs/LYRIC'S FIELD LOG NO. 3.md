**Woodland incident**
10/17 - AFTERNOON - NIGHT: NEIGHBORING WOODS
WITNESSES: ARNOLD FITZGERALD, MAE MORA

***Lead***:
> "Previously, going into the woods was not at all my intention, but I thought it necessary in order to protect our fellow campers. In fact, I'll put it out there, Mae Mora needs to be watched, she's been messing around with stuff she doesn't understand, and guaranteed she's gonna get herself killed, but that's besides the point"
> 
***The Trek***
> "Now, at no point was I alone, even before I went out after Mae I felt as if I was being watched. Though, in that regard I'll add that I Arnie followed me as I went out. We stayed to the trails, and of course we made sure to leave the near by foliage alone, but that didn't stop multiple Branchwalkers from watching us. 
***Incident(s)***
> Eventually, we did managed to find Mae, and we immediately attempted to pull her back to camp, but unfortunately for us, the Branchwalkers seemed to both usher us towards camp, and try to block us from getting out. Specifically, the Oak, Birch, Driftwood, and the black horned one seemed to either stop or push us towards the camp. Now, I will admit, the Horned one seemed to almost. . . . . Well ok, I can't explain it well, but I felt compelled to walk towards it, luckily my compadres pulled me back before anything could actually happen. One thing I will note, the Birchwalker seems to continue to show friendly or at least non-aggressive tendencies. It was one of the only one's to seem to usher us out of danger, even distracting the other more volatile Branchwalkers away from us so that we could cross the bridge. 
***Return to camp***
> Our trek back to camp wasn't as perilous as the way into the woods, but throughout the walk, I could tell in more ways than one that we we're being watched. I could see, hear, and feel them watching as we walked through the brush. Though, one notable encounter would be my interaction with the Branchwalker we have designated "Heimdall". It had blocked our way into camp, and as much as I wanted to, I found myself being sort of drawn to it, like something deep in my subconscious was urging me to go towards it, but luckily my compatriots pulled me back before I could get hurt. We did eventually get back to camp, thankfully. 
***Conclusion***
> Not sure what really to take from this, but this is one of my duties as a ARC member, i think, soooooo. . . Do what you will, just know that i aint going to the woods for a bit.
